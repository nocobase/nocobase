Preserve primary-key deferrability, index validity, partial-index restrictions, and index comparison compatibility during schema inspection and resolution. Keep optimistic single-row updates on the cardinality-checking path for generated or cascading locating keys, deferred or invalid constraints, unsafe partial or invalid indexes, and incompatible or unknown index equality semantics, including operator classes. Exclude PostgreSQL traditional inheritance parents whose unique keys do not cover child rows, while retaining eligible declaratively partitioned tables. Keep the preferred record identity instead of substituting a secondary unique key that a trigger could reassign, and diagnose unsuccessful writes using current rows even inside repeatable-read transactions. Roll back unsuccessful conditional writes, including statement-trigger effects from zero-row updates, before reporting the original missing-record or version-conflict error. Preserve earlier caller work and roll back writes that unexpectedly affect multiple rows or cannot reload their result. Keep Oracle character identities with session-dependent or unknown comparison semantics on the cardinality-checking path, without caching NLS settings or changing session behavior; numeric and explicitly binary character keys remain eligible. Associate constraints with their named backing indexes so comparison and validity metadata survive reordered or additional index keys, with order-independent column matching for unnamed references. Preserve all constraint references and deduplicate index keys when Oracle constraints share a backing index, so each constraint inherits its comparison and validity restrictions. Preserve usable Oracle local, global and composite partitioned unique indexes by checking partition and subpartition usability instead of treating their aggregate N/A status as invalid. Preserve SQL Server nullable unique identities whose index predicates only exclude null keys, and keep restricted unique indexes droppable through the Builder.
