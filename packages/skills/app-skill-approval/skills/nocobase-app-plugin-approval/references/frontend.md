# Implement the approval UI for the business

The approval plugin supplies server contracts and persisted approval work. Agents implement the frontend to match the requested business; there is no required page layout or automatic approval-center UI. Follow the application's frontend, theme, routing and permission conventions.

| Business experience                             | Implementation                                                                                                                                                          |
| ----------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Process approval from the business table/detail | Add submission, status and history to the existing business list/detail, and render the current person's offered task/run operations beside the reviewed content        |
| Process work in a dedicated to-do center        | Aggregate tasks across registered approvals, display business summaries and filtering, and open a task/record detail with its reviewed content, operations and timeline |
| Both                                            | Share the business detail and action components between the business table and the center; keep their operation contracts and displayed content consistent              |

Choose the form the user needs. A single business feature does not require a new cross-business center, and a center may still link to the underlying business record for context.

Read the current stage directly from the application lifecycle state. Aggregate all runs/tasks/events by lifecycle binding and application ID, including closed stays. Each run's `entryId` relates it to the application lifecycle log; there is no run lifecycle history to fetch. Old notification/task links show closed work and cannot act on a later stay. All approval-example scenarios use application lifecycles. Inspect `server/scenarios/application-bound.ts` for a small graph and `server/scenarios/contract-change.ts` for fact application and external retry.

## Routes behind the pages

The separately registered `@nocobase/app-plugin-task-center` ships authenticated personal-index reads at `/api/userTasks*`; see [User task center](user-tasks.md). Write business detail and approval operation routes in the business's own namespace following the application's HTTP API reference (`references/http-api.md` of the application development Skill): authentication and the business permission check before validation, `describeRoute` and `apiValidator` from `@nocobase/app-server/router`, refusals as `ApiError`. Two families serve an approval page:

- **Business transitions** — create, edit, submit, withdraw, resubmit, retry execution — are the application lifecycle's record routes (`view`, `fire` with `manual: true`, `create`), called from the page through `createLifecycleHook` in `@nocobase/lifecycle/react`. The hook sends the record version and a request key, and shows each blocker beside its button.
- **Approval operations** take a task or run: offered actions, `perform`, `preview`, `performBatch`, candidates, plus the read-model routes for lists and the detail. Resolve the actor from the session and check that the person may read the record before answering; `tasksFor()` and `runsFor()` are queries, not permissions. `involvementOf(lifecycle, recordId, person)` lists how the person takes part in the request — applicant, assignee, delegate or copied — so a copy recipient can be let in to follow its runs, timeline and steps while it is still under review; it is a fact to build the check on, not the check.

Declare who may read, process and administer the business as the application's authorization reference describes, and keep demonstration personas out of production routes. `packages/examples/app-plugin-approval-example/server/routes/lifecycle.ts` and `server/routes/api.ts` are complete examples of both families.

## Pages to build

| Page                      | Content                                                                                                                                                                         |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| New request               | The business form, `previewRoute` beside it showing who each stage would ask, submit through the lifecycle with its blockers shown                                              |
| My requests               | The applicant's applications by state and latest update, with withdraw and resubmit where offered                                                                               |
| To do                     | Personal index filtered by `status=pending` and `sourceType=approval.task`, with business detail and offered batch actions loaded through authorized source APIs                |
| Done and copied to me     | Personal index filtered by completed status or `sourceType=approval.cc`, with the copy read action supplied by its business API                                                 |
| Detail                    | The reviewed content with its `contentHash`, progress with upcoming stages, the timeline, the action bar from offered task and run actions, and execution status after approval |
| Administration, if needed | Stages holding unassigned places to appoint, reassignment, and failed or waiting effect runs (`listEffectRuns`) with retry                                                      |

Each list links to the detail by lifecycle and record ID, so a notice, a list row and a shared link open the same view.

## Data and operation contract

- For business detail, use `actionsFor(lifecycle, recordId, actor)` and `runActionsFor(lifecycle, recordId, actor)` to obtain offered operations, and `runViewsFor(lifecycle, recordId)` for each run with its stages, tasks, events, timeline and return targets; `upcomingStagesFor(lifecycle, recordId, actor)` adds the stages still ahead, and `timelineFor(lifecycle, recordId)` is the timeline alone. Before submission, `previewRoute({ lifecycle, record, actor, applicantId?, submit? })` shows who each stage would ask; it writes nothing. Distinguish business submission/withdrawal transitions from task/run actions; submission goes through the business lifecycle.
- Every offered action carries `tone`, `primary` and `contentHash` (whether it is refused without the run's content hash) as its definition declares them. Draw controls from those, never from a list of action names, so an action another plugin adds is drawn correctly.
- For personal center lists/counts, use [the user task index](user-tasks.md) for candidate discovery. The host owns business scope, complete discovery (including business record supplementation when projections are absent or lagging), authorization and post-action remaining checks. Verify candidates through each business record's `tasksFor()` and `runsFor()`, including original assignee, task kind/role, OPEN status and current run/stage/version. Execute explicit targets through the owner's business action or `performBatch()`. See [the breaking API migration](task-handover-migration.md).
- Display the actual content under review and retain its `contentHash` for decisions. Render offered reason requirements, return targets, signer modes and revision fields. Use `candidatesFor` for operation-specific people pickers; paginate through `nextOffset`. Add a business-specific form for a custom action's input rather than assuming the server generates a form schema.
- Execute through authenticated host routes calling `perform` (or `performBatch` for supported batch actions), with actors derived server-side. `preview` may support a review step; on a batch, display the individual outcomes. After an operation, refresh the record, tasks, offered actions and history so completed work cannot remain actionable. Do not write task/run tables from the browser.
- Provide labels/translations and an optional timeline renderer for custom actions, registered with `registerTimelineRenderer` from `@nocobase/app-plugin-approval/client`; the plugin registers its built-in actions' renderers itself. Adding a server action does not add a production form. Keep the server's offered operations authoritative and its submission checks intact.

## The to-do center has several sources

Task Center provides the generic list and source extension points; the host owns business detail/action adapters and page composition. Register each source with the shared [user task index](user-tasks.md); query its owner-scoped list and counts so filtering, ordering and pagination happen once in the database. Non-approval work — a checklist, failed execution step or notice to confirm — stays in its own business records and lifecycle and contributes through its own writer. Approval work opts into the definition's `userTasks` mapping. Applicant tracking is another host-owned source.

Render safe indexed titles, then load authorized source-specific detail and offered actions when opening an item. An approval item uses its task ID (`sourceKey`) with the business approval API; an ordinary work item uses its business lifecycle. Refresh the index and detail after successful operations. Index ownership is not action authorization. The example's `server/scenarios/inbox.ts` demonstrates the older source aggregation contract; do not copy its per-source list merge into a new personal center.

## Ready-made components

Install the UI Library's `approval-ui` item into the application (`pnpm dlx shadcn@latest add @nocobase/approval-ui`) rather than drawing progress, history, a route preview or an action bar from scratch. Its components — `ApprovalProgress`, `ApprovalTimeline`, `ApprovalRoutePreview`, `ApprovalActionBar`, `ApprovalBranches`, `ApprovalReceipts` and `ApprovalUiProvider`, in `client/extensions/nocobase-approval-ui/` — depend on no plugin: they take display shapes from its `types.ts` with every label already translated. Write the mapping beside the page: build steps with `approvalStepsOf` and `approvalCopiesOf`, history lines with `renderTimelineEntry`, words with `approvalWord`, and bar actions from `offeredActions` (each action's `tone`, `primary` and `contentHash`), all from `@nocobase/app-plugin-approval/client`. A step several people decide gets a `tally` from its tasks' answers and its `rule` (a vote's `min` approvals and `vetoPlaces`, the keys of its veto pools) and, for a stage with pools, the step's `pools` (each pool's tasks, shown with `ApprovalBranches`) and `answers` (such as `abstain`); an open task's `dueAt` becomes its `due`; bookkeeping lines are `muted` so the timeline folds them. `packages/examples/app-plugin-approval-example/client/center/approval-views.ts` is a complete mapping. Name people through the provider, render each action's own form through the bar's `form.render`, and merge the item's `locales/` and the plugin's `approvalLocaleEnUS`/`approvalLocaleZhCN` into the application's locale resources. The installed files belong to the application; keep business-specific displays such as list rows, field grids and record content in the application's own components.

## Shape the page by business

Businesses differ in more than fields, so decide per business how its list, facts, progress and actions look instead of one row-and-panel template with a different field list. Put the fact an approver decides on — the amount, the days and dates, the counterparty — in the list's main column, and use a table with a total where people handle amounts in bulk. Show a vote's count, pass mark and veto; parallel branches as a whole with who holds each and which one blocks (`ApprovalBranches`); a notice as recipients with read and confirmed counts (`ApprovalReceipts`), not as an approval; and a wait for an outside system apart from a wait for a person. Let approval take an optional comment, required only where the business says so, and ask before an action that moves money. Offer the return targets with their meaning and say which opinions a return keeps. Offer batch approval through `performBatch` with each target's `contentHash`, and move on to the next to-do once one is handled. Keep demonstration scaffolding — personas, guides, sample loading — collapsed and out of the way of the person's work.
